Business or company? The legal difference in Guyana

A registered business name

Business Names (Registration) Act, Cap. 90:05

  • For a sole trader or a partnership trading under a name other than the owners’ own names.
  • Not a separate legal person. In law, you and the business are the same.
  • You are personally liable for all of the business’s debts, without limit.
  • Must be registered with DCRA within 14 days of starting business.
  • Quicker, simpler and cheaper to set up and run.

An incorporated company

Companies Act, Cap. 89:01

  • A separate legal person, distinct from the people who own and run it.
  • Owns property, signs contracts, and sues or is sued in its own name.
  • Shareholders’ liability is limited, usually to what they have paid or agreed to pay for their shares.
  • Carries on when shareholders or directors change.
  • Needs directors, a registered office in Guyana and annual returns to the Registrar.

In short: a business name is a trading name for you; a company is a new legal person that you own. This is general information, not legal advice. If you’re not sure which suits you, ask us or an attorney-at-law.
